## Subway

You can tap a contactless credit/debit card or a phone with a mobile wallet at the subway entrance. You don't need to buy a MetroCard to use this payment method.

Use Google Maps for directions, and check MTA service updates for changes, especially on weekends. Do not promise that a particular route or schedule is currently operating unless connected to current service information.

Source: [MTA OMNY tap guidance](https://www.mta.info/document/107486). [MTA](https://www.mta.info/) is the primary service-update source. Do not hardcode fares or fare-cap amounts in v1.

## Reservations

Popular restaurants can need planning ahead. Check the restaurant's official reservation or walk-in policy before going.

This is general guidance. Do not place a "Reservation required" badge on every food card. For example, [Bar Pisellino's official site](https://barpisellino.com/) explicitly says no reservations.

## Getting around from Greenpoint

The NYC Ferry is one option for reaching Manhattan, and the G train is useful for connections around Brooklyn and Queens. Check the route and service for your destination and time of travel.

For NYC Ferry, buy a ticket before boarding. The app is convenient, but ticket machines at ferry landings are also available and accept credit cards. Don't describe ferry boarding as the same bank-card tap used at a subway turnstile.

Sources: [NYC Ferry FAQ](https://www.ferry.nyc/about/faq/), [ticket information](https://www.ferry.nyc/ticketing-info/). Avoid hardcoded fares, transfer durations, and departure times; some official pages have inconsistent older text, so use current route/ticket pages when needed.

## Walks Eda specifically mentioned

- Brooklyn Bridge: walk **Brooklyn → Manhattan**.
- High Line: combine it with Chelsea Market, Meatpacking, or West Village.
- Hudson River Park / West Side: especially nice around sunset.
- Greenpoint waterfront: Transmitter Park → Greenpoint Ferry.

These are personal suggestions, not turn-by-turn navigation or live operating information.
